What TOCForge PDF does, and why it matters

Generate a table-of-contents page from a PDF's headings and insert it at the front, with page numbers, as a new PDF. The job at the heart of this tool is giving a long report, thesis or manual the contents page it always needed, so readers can navigate instead of scrolling and guessing. That sounds narrow until you are the person facing it at a deadline, discovering that a general office suite either cannot do it, hides it behind a paid tier, or does it in a way that quietly changes something else in the document along the way.

A dedicated tool earns its keep by making the one job obvious: choose your file, set the few options that genuinely matter, run it, and check the result. A PDF earns its place because it looks the same on every device, printer and operating system, which is why contracts, reports, certificates and manuals all travel in this format even when they were created in something else entirely. The catch is that a format designed for faithful display is not naturally easy to change, search, split or reuse, and that gap between reading a document and actually working with it is exactly the gap a focused single-purpose tool is meant to close. When the task is giving a long report, those are not abstract virtues — they decide whether the file you produce is the file the other person can actually open, read and file without a follow-up conversation.

Like every tool in this collection, TOCForge PDF is free without signup, paywalls or watermarks, and it runs entirely in your browser. That combination matters most for exactly the documents people use this kind of tool on: statements, certificates, applications, contracts and work files that deserve privacy by default rather than privacy as a premium feature you have to remember to switch on.

How it works, step by step

Start with the file or content panel at the top of this page. For file-based work, choose the document from your device; nothing is uploaded, and the tool reads it locally using the same proven PDF libraries that power many desktop utilities. Set the options that apply to your job — they are labelled in plain language, and the defaults are chosen for the most common case, so most people can run the tool exactly as it first appears.

Press the main button and watch the status line, which reports progress honestly as pages are processed rather than spinning silently. When the work finishes, the result appears in the output area, the report area or as a ready download, depending on the job. Good document work is mostly about trust and checking: glance at the result before you send it, keep your original untouched as the master copy, and treat every converted or rebuilt file as a new edition that deserves ten seconds of review. Before you rely on the output, open it once and check the pages that matter most: the first page, a middle page, and any page you know is unusual, such as a wide table, a signed page or a dense image. That ten-second check is the professional difference between hoping a conversion worked and knowing that it worked, and it costs almost nothing compared with re-sending a corrected file after a deadline.

Tips for the best results

Work from the best source you have. A born-digital PDF exported from its original application will almost always give cleaner results than a scan, and a clean scan — square-on, well lit, at a sensible resolution — will beat a hurried photograph every time. When the input is a photograph of a page, straighten and crop it first if you can, because no downstream tool can fully undo a tilted, shadowed starting point, however hard it tries on your behalf.

Keep your original file untouched and let the tool produce a new copy, every time, without exception. That single habit makes every experiment free: if an option was wrong, a page looks off, or a setting needs another attempt, you simply run the tool again from the pristine source instead of trying to unpick changes layered on changes. Small habits compound quickly here, because naming outputs clearly, keeping originals separate from working copies and noting which version went to whom will save more real-world trouble than any single clever feature ever could on its own. Professionals version their documents for the same reason developers version code — memory is fallible, and files do not argue back.

Match the tool settings to the destination, not to habit. A file headed for a portal with a size cap, a printer with bleed requirements, an e-reader with a small screen and an archive meant to last a decade are four different destinations, and the right choices for each are different too. Reading the option labels with the destination in mind takes seconds and prevents the most common document problems, which are rarely technical failures and usually mismatches between a file and where it was going.

Privacy, limits and honest notes

Privacy here is architectural, not a promise buried in a policy. The code runs in your browser tab, the file is processed in your device's memory, and closing the tab ends the session with nothing stored anywhere by this tool. Accessibility and readability deserve a mention in any document workflow, because clear structure, real text rather than pictures of text, and sensible page order help every reader, including people using search, translation, read-aloud and assistive technology. For the documents people most often process — identity proofs, bank statements, signed agreements, medical and legal papers — that design is the difference between a tool you can use confidently and one you should hesitate over.

Honesty about limits matters just as much. Browser processing is bounded by your device's memory, so extremely large files may be kinder split or handled in batches. Some transformations are inherently lossy or structural — rendering a page preserves how it looks, not how its text is stored; extracting text preserves words, not layout — and the tool's own notes call those trade-offs out where they apply, so you can choose with your eyes open instead of discovering them in the output.

The practical rule is simple: use TOCForge PDF for the job it describes, check the result once, and keep your master original safe. Do that, and a free browser tool will handle a surprising share of the document work that once needed paid software, a trip to a print shop, or a favour from the one colleague who knew how — which is precisely the point of putting it here, unlimited and open, for anyone who needs it.
